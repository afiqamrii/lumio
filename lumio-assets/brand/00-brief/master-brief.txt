Create a complete visual brand identity system for a children's educational game app called **Lumio**.

## 1. Brand overview

**Brand name:** Lumio

**Tagline:** Play. Learn. Glow.

**Product:** A mobile educational game app for children aged approximately 5–9.

Lumio combines learning and play through animated mini-games, adventure worlds, rewards, progression, and a friendly mascot.

The learning areas include:
- Mathematics
- English
- Bahasa Melayu
- Arabic
- Logic and problem solving
- General knowledge / science

The product should feel like an actual game children want to play, while still looking trustworthy and valuable to parents.

## 2. Brand positioning

Lumio represents:

**Light + curiosity + discovery + growth + learning through play.**

The core idea is:

> Every time a child learns, their world becomes brighter.

The brand should feel:
- playful
- friendly
- imaginative
- intelligent
- safe
- warm
- modern
- premium
- energetic
- highly memorable

Avoid making it:
- overly childish or babyish
- generic preschool branding
- overly academic
- corporate
- cluttered
- overstimulating
- derivative of existing educational brands

The identity must feel original and ownable.

## 3. Target audience

### Primary user
Children aged 5–9.

The branding must be exciting and recognisable to children.

### Buyer
Parents.

Parents should perceive Lumio as:
- educational
- trustworthy
- polished
- safe
- worth paying for

The visual system therefore needs to balance playful child appeal with a professional, premium feel.

## 4. Mascot

Create an original mascot called:

# Lumi

Lumi is Lumio's main companion and guide.

Lumi should NOT simply be a normal existing animal such as an owl, panda, cat, rabbit, or bear.

Instead, Lumi should be an original **glowing light-sprite creature** with subtle animal-like qualities.

Lumi represents:
- curiosity
- light
- encouragement
- discovery
- friendship

### Physical design direction

Lumi should have:
- a simple, recognisable silhouette
- soft rounded proportions
- large expressive eyes
- small body
- expressive hands or tiny arms
- playful movement potential
- a glowing element
- a signature visual feature

Possible signature features:
- glowing forehead spark
- glowing tail tip
- floating light orb
- star-shaped glow
- glowing chest mark
- small luminous antenna

Choose ONE clear signature feature rather than combining too many details.

Lumi should look easy to animate.

The mascot must support expressions such as:
- happy
- excited
- curious
- thinking
- surprised
- proud
- confused
- sleepy
- celebrating

Lumi should feel lovable enough that children could eventually want:
- stickers
- plush toys
- merchandise
- profile avatars

Avoid making Lumi look:
- creepy
- overly detailed
- photorealistic
- like an existing famous character
- like a generic stock mascot

## 5. Logo concepts required

Produce a complete logo family rather than one logo only.

Create all of the following:

### A. Primary combination logo
Lumio wordmark + Lumi mascot.

This is the main brand logo.

Explore compositions such as:
- Lumi next to the wordmark
- Lumi sitting above one of the letters
- Lumi interacting with the letter “i”
- Lumi peeking through or around the “o”

Keep the composition simple and professional.

### B. Typography-only logo
Create a standalone **Lumio** wordmark without the mascot.

The wordmark should:
- use rounded custom typography
- feel friendly and modern
- remain readable at small sizes
- have slightly playful letterforms
- avoid looking like generic bubble lettering

Explore whether:
- the dot of the “i” becomes a glowing spark
- the “o” becomes a subtle light orb

Do not overdecorate the typography.

### C. Mascot-only mark
Create a standalone simplified Lumi icon.

It must work for:
- app icon
- favicon
- social avatar
- loading screen
- stickers

The silhouette should remain recognisable even at very small sizes.

### D. Symbol-only logo
Explore a simplified abstract Lumio symbol based on:
- light
- spark
- glow
- learning
- discovery

It must NOT simply be a generic lightbulb.

Possible inspiration:
- glowing orb
- spark
- luminous path
- abstract “L”
- star-like learning symbol

### E. Horizontal logo
Lumi + Lumio wordmark in a wide horizontal layout.

### F. Vertical logo
Lumi above the Lumio wordmark.

### G. Monochrome logo
Create black-only and white-only versions suitable for printing and accessibility.

## 6. App icon

Create a highly recognisable app icon.

Preferred direction:

Lumi's face or simplified head on a bold clean background.

Requirements:
- readable at small mobile sizes
- no tiny details
- strong silhouette
- friendly facial expression
- instantly recognisable
- looks good on both Android and iOS
- no text inside the icon unless absolutely necessary

The icon should not look like a generic educational app.

## 7. Color palette

Create a complete brand palette.

### Suggested primary direction

#### Lumio Glow
Warm golden yellow.

Represents:
- light
- achievement
- discovery
- happiness

#### Lumio Sky
Friendly blue or turquoise.

Represents:
- trust
- openness
- calm
- technology

### Supporting colors

Introduce controlled supporting colors such as:
- coral
- soft purple
- green
- warm cream
- dark navy / charcoal

Avoid using every bright color equally.

The palette should feel cohesive and intentional.

Provide:
- primary colors
- secondary colors
- neutral colors
- HEX values
- RGB values

Also explain what each color should be used for.

## 8. Typography system

Recommend a typography system for Lumio.

Need:
- playful display typeface for headings
- highly readable UI typeface
- appropriate numeric type for maths games

Typography must:
- support children learning to read
- have clear letterforms
- avoid confusing lowercase characters
- work well for English and Bahasa Melayu

Consider future Arabic-script compatibility in the broader UI system.

Avoid overly decorative fonts for educational content.

## 9. Illustration style

Define an illustration system.

Recommended characteristics:
- rounded shapes
- soft depth
- bright but controlled colors
- simple shading
- expressive characters
- clean outlines or outline-free soft vector style
- subtle gradients where appropriate

The style should work for:
- game characters
- maps
- animals
- objects
- buildings
- rewards
- educational illustrations

Avoid:
- photorealism
- overly complex textures
- generic stock illustration
- excessive visual clutter

## 10. Animation language

Define how the brand should move.

Lumio is an animation-rich product.

Animation principles:
- squash and stretch
- soft bouncing
- quick anticipation
- glowing trails
- spark particles
- smooth world transitions
- celebratory micro-animations
- expressive mascot reactions

Interactions should feel responsive.

Examples:
- button slightly compresses when tapped
- correct answer creates a short glow pulse
- stars fly into the progress counter
- Lumi jumps when a skill is completed
- unlocking a world creates a gentle burst of light

Avoid excessive animation that distracts from learning.

## 11. Sound identity

Define a high-level sound branding direction.

Lumio should have a recognisable audio identity.

Include ideas for:
- app startup chime
- correct-answer sound
- wrong-answer sound
- reward sound
- world unlock sound
- transition sound
- Lumi vocal reactions

Lumi's vocal reactions can be short non-verbal sounds such as:
- “ooh!”
- “hmm!”
- “yay!”
- surprised gasp

Keep sounds:
- warm
- short
- non-annoying
- pleasant after repeated use

## 13. UI design direction

Create example UI directions for:

### Child home screen
- character / mascot
- world map
- daily learning goal
- stars
- coins
- simple navigation

### Game screen
- minimal distractions
- large touch targets
- clear question
- interactive content
- mascot feedback
- progression indicator

### Level complete screen
- celebratory animation
- stars
- rewards
- progress
- next mission

### Parent dashboard
Use a calmer visual treatment.

Show:
- learning time
- progress
- strengths
- skills requiring practice
- subject breakdown
- child profiles

Parent mode should feel clearly connected to Lumio while being more mature and data-focused.

## 14. Accessibility

Brand design must support:
- strong contrast
- clear text
- large touch targets
- color not being the only source of information
- readable typography
- reduced-motion mode
- sound-off usability

Do not rely solely on red/green for correct and incorrect responses.

## 15. Brand tone

Lumio's communication style should be:

Encouraging:
“Great try!”

Curious:
“What happens if we try this?”

Celebratory:
“You unlocked something new!”

Supportive:
“Almost there. Let's try again.”

Never shame the child.

Avoid:
- “WRONG!”
- “FAILED!”
- “You lost!”
- harsh negative sounds

## 17. Important originality requirement

Do not reproduce, imitate, trace, or closely resemble:
- Duolingo
- Khan Academy Kids
- ABCmouse
- Lingokids
- Pok Pok
- Sago Mini
- or other existing educational brands.

Use them only as general market context regarding quality and polish.

Lumio must have its own:
- silhouette
- visual personality
- mascot
- typography
- color language
- brand world

The final result should feel like a brand that could stand independently beside major children's apps.

## 18. Final creative direction

The overall feeling should be:

**A magical learning universe powered by curiosity and light.**

A child should see Lumio and think:

> “I want to play this.”

A parent should see Lumio and think:

> “This looks safe, educational, thoughtful, and professionally made.”

The brand should be suitable for long-term expansion into:
- mobile apps
- tablets
- web
- educational content
- books
- stickers
- plush toys
- merchandise
- schools
- global markets

Create multiple distinct visual concepts first, then recommend the strongest direction while preserving Lumio's originality.
